# CompTIA Security+ Notes: 5.2 Risk Management

This section covers how organizations identify, evaluate, and respond to risks, as well as the metrics used to track system reliability and recovery.

## 1. Risk Assessments

Risk assessments help organizations determine which threats require immediate attention and funding.

### Qualitative Risk Assessment
* **Concept:** A subjective approach based on judgment, intuition, and experience rather than exact financial figures. 
* **Method:** Categorizes risks into tiers (e.g., Low, Medium, High, Critical).
* **Risk Matrix (Heatmap):** A visual tool that plots the *Likelihood* (probability of the event occurring) against the *Impact* (damage if it does occur). A high-likelihood, high-impact event is flagged red (critical).

### Quantitative Risk Assessment
* **Concept:** An objective approach that assigns real dollar values to risk. It calculates the exact financial impact to help justify the budget for security controls.
* **The Formula:** You **must** know these terms and how to calculate them for the exam:
  * **SLE (Single Loss Expectancy):** The financial cost of *one single incident*. (e.g., A stolen laptop costs $2,000).
  * **ARO (Annualized Rate of Occurrence):** How many times you expect this incident to happen in one year. (e.g., You expect 5 laptops to be stolen per year).
  * **ALE (Annualized Loss Expectancy):** The total expected financial loss per year.
  * **Equation:** **ALE = SLE × ARO** (e.g., $2,000 × 5 = $10,000 ALE). 
  * *Application:* If the ALE is $10,000, you should not spend $15,000 a year on a security control to prevent it (the control costs more than the risk).

## 2. Risk Treatment Options

Once a risk is identified, management must choose how to respond to it. 

* **Accept:** The risk is minor, or the cost of fixing it is higher than the potential damage. The organization documents the risk and does nothing, choosing to absorb the potential loss.
* **Avoid:** The risk is too high, and no control can reduce it to a safe level. The organization completely stops the activity that causes the risk (e.g., shutting down a legacy application, refusing to do business in a high-risk country).
* **Mitigate (Reduce):** The most common response. The organization implements security controls (firewalls, patching, training) to lower the risk's likelihood or impact to an acceptable level.
* **Transfer (Share):** The organization shifts the financial burden of the risk to a third party. The primary example is buying **Cyber Insurance**, or outsourcing a risky process to a specialized cloud provider.

## 3. Metrics & Register

These tools and metrics help track risk posture and operational resilience.

### Tracking Risk
* **Risk Register:** A master, living document (often a spreadsheet or database) that tracks every identified risk in the organization. It details the risk description, its qualitative/quantitative score, who owns it, and how it is currently being treated.
* **KRIs (Key Risk Indicators):** Specific metrics used to predict the likelihood of a risk materializing. While a KPI measures performance, a KRI measures danger (e.g., A high number of unpatched critical servers is a KRI indicating an impending breach).

### Reliability and Recovery Metrics
* **RTO (Recovery Time Objective):** The maximum allowable time a system can be offline before the business suffers catastrophic failure. (e.g., "The e-commerce site must be back online within 4 hours.")
* **RPO (Recovery Point Objective):** The maximum allowable data loss, measured in time. (e.g., "We backup every 24 hours, so our RPO is 24 hours of lost data.")
* **MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures):** A reliability metric measuring the average time a system runs continuously before it breaks down. **Higher is better.** (Used to predict when hardware will need replacing).
* **MTTR (Mean Time to Repair/Recovery):** A recovery metric measuring the average time it takes to fix a broken system and restore it to full functionality. **Lower is better.**

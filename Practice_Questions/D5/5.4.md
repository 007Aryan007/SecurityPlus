# CompTIA Security+ Notes: 5.4 Security Compliance & Privacy

This section covers the major regulations and core privacy principles that dictate how organizations must handle, store, and protect sensitive data. Failing to comply can result in massive financial penalties and legal action.

## 1. Regulatory Oversight

Organizations must adhere to various laws and industry standards depending on their location and the type of data they handle.

### GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

* **Scope:** A comprehensive privacy law enacted by the European Union (EU).
* **Key Concept:** It protects the personal data of EU citizens, **regardless of where the company processing the data is located in the world**. If a US company has EU customers, they must comply with GDPR.
* **Impact:** Gives immense control to the user over their data (requires explicit consent for data collection) and carries massive financial penalties for breaches (up to 4% of global global revenue).

### HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)

* **Scope:** A United States federal law.
* **Key Concept:** Mandates the protection and confidential handling of **PHI (Protected Health Information)**. It applies to "covered entities" like hospitals, doctors, insurance providers, and their third-party business associates.
* **Impact:** Requires strict access controls, encryption, and audit logging to ensure patient records are not improperly accessed or leaked.

### PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)

* **Scope:** A global industry standard, **not a government law**. Created by major credit card companies (Visa, Mastercard, etc.).
* **Key Concept:** Applies to any organization that accepts, transmits, or stores credit card data (Cardholder Data / CHD).
* **Impact:** Requires merchants to implement specific security controls (like tokenization, encryption, firewalls, and regular vulnerability scans). Non-compliance can result in a business losing its ability to process credit cards.

### SOC 2 (Service Organization Control Type 2) Compliance Reports

* **Scope:** An auditing standard created by the American Institute of CPAs (AICPA).
* **Key Concept:** It evaluates a cloud service provider or third-party vendor's security based on five "Trust Services Criteria": Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy.
* **Impact:** If your company is hiring a cloud vendor to host sensitive data, you will request their SOC 2 report to verify that an independent auditor has confirmed their security controls are actually functioning as promised.

## 2. Privacy Principles

Privacy is not just about keeping hackers out (security); it is about respecting the user's rights and handling their data ethically and legally.

### Data Minimization

* **Principle:** An organization should only collect the absolute minimum amount of personal data necessary to accomplish a specific, stated purpose.
* **Example:** If you are signing up for a simple email newsletter, the company only needs your email address. They should not force you to provide your phone number, home address, or date of birth.

### Data Sovereignty

* **Principle:** Data is subject to the laws and legal structures of the specific nation/country where it is physically stored or processed.
* **Security Implication:** If a US company stores its data in a data center located in Germany, that data is now subject to German privacy laws. This forces companies to be very careful about where they build servers or which cloud regions they select.

### Right to be Forgotten (Right to Erasure)

* **Principle:** A core tenant of GDPR. It gives individuals the right to request that an organization completely and permanently delete all their personal data from the organization's servers and databases.
* **Challenge:** Can be technically difficult for IT teams to implement, as they must hunt down the user's data across active databases, backups, and third-party shared systems to ensure it is entirely wiped out.

### Privacy Impact Assessments (PIA)

* **Principle:** A formal, documented process used to identify and mitigate privacy risks *before* a new project or system is launched.
* **Function:** When a company builds a new application that will collect user data, a PIA is performed to answer: What data is being collected? Why? Who has access to it? How long is it kept? It ensures privacy is "baked in" by design rather than added as an afterthought.

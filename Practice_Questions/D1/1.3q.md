#### PBQ 1: Change Request Lifecycle and CAB Approval Workflow

**Scenario:**
A lead database administrator submits a change request (CR) to upgrade a central customer-facing PostgreSQL database engine from version 13 to version 16 across the production cluster.

Below is an unordered list of procedural steps involved in the formal Change Management lifecycle.

**Tasks:**

1. **Sequence Order:** Place the steps in the correct chronological order (from 1 to 8) according to standard corporate governance and Change Advisory Board (CAB) best practices.

2. **Missing Prerequisite Identification:** Identify which vital technical safeguard is missing or poorly defined in the submission based on the details provided.

**Lifecycle Steps to Sequence:**

* **Step A:** Deploy the upgrade binaries to an isolated sandbox environment that mirrors production data scale to assess performance and compatibility.

* **Step B:** Implement the upgrade during the pre-approved scheduled maintenance maintenance window.

* **Step C:** Formally present the change proposal, business justification, and risk assessment to the Change Advisory Board (CAB) for evaluation.

* **Step D:** Conduct a post-implementation review (PIR) and document lessons learned.

* **Step E:** Draft a detailed backout/rollback plan with specific trigger thresholds and maximum tolerable downtime limits.

* **Step F:** Submit the formal Request for Change (RFC) documentation detailing system scope, dependencies, and business impact.

* **Step G:** CAB and designated executive stakeholders approve the change and assign a deployment window.

* **Step H:** Perform operational sanity checks, smoke testing, and data integrity verification immediately following deployment.

**Questions for PBQ 1:**

* **1.1:** What is the correct 8-step sequence? (`___ -> ___ -> ___ -> ___ -> ___ -> ___ -> ___ -> ___`)

* **1.2:** If Step H reveals that 35% of database queries are experiencing deadlocks and latency violates the Service Level Agreement (SLA), what predefined procedural mechanism must immediately execute, and what technical artifact must it rely on?

### Section 2: Scenario-Based Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

#### Question 1

A DevOps engineer discovers a zero-day remote code execution vulnerability in the production web server fleet on a Friday afternoon. The standard Change Advisory Board (CAB) convenes only on Tuesday mornings. Company policy requires rapid remediation for critical threats while maintaining operational integrity.

Which of the following processes should the engineer invoke?

* A. Request an emergency change approval (ECAB) and document the remediation retroactively or expeditiously according to policy.

* B. Wait until the standard Tuesday CAB meeting to ensure full stakeholder alignment.

* C. Manually apply the patch immediately to production without logging a change request to minimize the window of exposure.

* D. Revert the web servers to a previous sandbox image without notifying system owners.

#### Question 2

During a scheduled operating system upgrade on a cluster of domain controllers, the replication service fails, preventing user authentications across several branch offices. The maintenance window is set to expire in 15 minutes.

Which of the following technical artifacts should the systems engineer consult immediately?

* A. Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

* B. Rollback / Backout plan

* C. Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

* D. Data Loss Prevention (DLP) rulebase

#### Question 3

Which of the following technical implications of change management is being enforced?

* A. Downtime scheduling

* B. Version control

* C. Dynamic sandboxing

* D. Business continuity planning

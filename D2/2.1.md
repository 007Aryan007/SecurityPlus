## Domain 2.0: Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations (22%)
### Objective 2.1: Compare and contrast common threat actors and motivations

## 1. Threat Actor Attributes & Types

### 1.1 Nation-States & Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)
* **Nation-States (State-Sponsored Actors):**
  * **Backing:** Directed, protected, or funded directly by national governments and intelligence/military services.
  * **Capabilities & Resources:** Near-unlimited financial and computational resources, top-tier technical expertise, and custom zero-day exploit pipelines.
  * **Primary Targets:** Critical infrastructure (power grids, water supplies, transit), defense industrial bases, rival government agencies, and strategic multinational corporations.
* **Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs):**
  * **Advanced:** Possess custom-developed malware, living-off-the-land techniques, and deep technical evasion capabilities.
  * **Persistent:** Focus on **stealth and extended dwell time**. They covertly maintain Command and Control (C2) channels over weeks, months, or years without triggering alerts.
  * **Threat:** Highly targeted against specific entities for intelligence extraction rather than fast opportunistic payouts.
* **Exam Key:** Look for scenarios emphasizing unlimited resources, deep geopolitical motives, stealthy persistent presence, or intellectual property theft.

---

### 1.2 Unskilled Attackers & Hacktivists
* **Unskilled Attackers (Script Kiddies):**
  * **Definition:** Attackers who lack deep programming knowledge or a foundational understanding of underlying vulnerabilities and exploit internals.
  * **Tactics:** Exclusively deploy pre-packaged scripts, automated exploit tools (e.g., automated DDoS tools, public scripts) found online or on forums.
  * **Drivers:** Curiosity, ego, bragging rights, or random vandalism.
* **Hacktivists:**
  * **Definition:** Individuals or groups acting on specific political, ideological, social, religious, or environmental causes.
  * **Tactics:** Focus on high-visibility disruption—website defacements, Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) downtime, and doxxing (publishing confidential documents to embarrass the target).
  * **Drivers:** Philosophical and political messaging; personal financial profit is rarely their objective.

---

### 1.3 Insider Threats
* **Definition:** Threats originating from current or former employees, contractors, managed service providers, or trusted partners who have legitimate, authorized access to organizational infrastructure.
* **Core Danger:**
  * Already inside the security perimeter (bypassing boundary firewalls and external WAFs).
  * Possess institutional knowledge of data storage locations, access controls, weak points, and blind spots.
* **Categories:**
  1. **Malicious Insider:** Intentionally abuses credentials for financial gain, revenge (disgruntled employees), corporate espionage, or sabotage.
  2. **Accidental / Negligent Insider:** Well-meaning employees who trigger incidents through negligence (e.g., clicking phishing links, misconfiguring cloud storage buckets to public read, falling for pretexting).
* **CompTIA Mitigation Controls:**
  * **Least Privilege:** Restrict user accounts to the bare minimum permissions needed to execute their specific job duties.
  * **Separation of Duties & Dual Control:** Ensure critical operations require more than one person to initiate and approve.
  * **Mandatory Vacations:** Requires employees to take consecutive time off while access is revoked; substitutes step in, exposing ongoing fraudulent manipulation.
  * **Job Rotation:** Rotates personnel through critical administrative roles, deterring ongoing schemes and uncovering unauthorized configurations.
  * **Robust Offboarding:** Standard operating procedures to immediately revoke access (Active Directory, SSO, VPN, email) and retrieve physical assets (hardware, security tokens, badges) upon separation.

---

### 1.4 Organized Crime Syndicates
* **Definition:** Professional, highly organized criminal enterprises operating illegal operations structured like commercial corporations.
* **Key Features:**
  * **Substantial Resources:** Dedicated departments for malware development, money laundering, affiliate coordination, and extortion negotiation.
  * **Cybercrime-as-a-Service (CaaS):** The commercialization of illicit capabilities on dark web marketplaces. Turnkey phishing kits, botnet access, and exploit infrastructure rented to lower-skilled buyers.
  * **Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS):** Core authors build the ransomware payload and infrastructure, taking a 20–30% cut of extortion payments, while independent "affiliates" breach networks and execute deployments for a 70–80% share.
* **Common Attack Vectors:**
  * **POS Skimming:** Installing physical overlays on card readers/ATMs or deploying memory-scraping malware onto retail cash registers to intercept card details.
  * **Banking Trojans:** Specialized malware (e.g., *Zeus*, *Qakbot*) executing browser injections or session hijacking to steal banking logins and OTP tokens.
  * **Coordinated Wire Fraud (BEC):** Business Email Compromise using executive impersonation or vendor invoice modification to redirect corporate wire transfers to money mule networks.
* **Primary Motivation:** **Direct Financial Gain.**

---

### 1.5 Shadow IT
* **Definition:** Hardware, software, applications, or cloud services implemented and maintained by employees or business departments without the approval, visibility, or oversight of centralized IT/Security.
* **Context:** Usually driven by convenience or productivity bottlenecks rather than malice (e.g., an employee using personal cloud storage because internal transfer tools are slow).
* **Security Risks:**
  * **Blind Spots:** Security teams cannot monitor, patch, or evaluate risks they are unaware of.
  * **Data Exfiltration & Compliance Breaches:** Unencrypted corporate or client data living outside DLP and governance perimeters.
* **Mitigation Controls:**
  * **Cloud Access Security Broker (CASB):** Monitored proxies/gateways enforcing security policies and uncovering unsanctioned cloud app usage.
  * **Network Access Control (NAC):** Prevents unauthorized physical or wireless devices from joining enterprise subnets.

## 2. Threat Actor Motivations

| Motivation | Objective & Description | Primary Indicators & Techniques | Typical Threat Actor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Gain** | Accumulating direct monetary profit or extortion funds. | Ransomware deployment, credit card skimming, BEC wire fraud, cryptojacking, dark web data selling. | Organized Crime, Financially Motivated Insiders |
| **Data Exfiltration** | Covertly extracting or copying proprietary, confidential, or sensitive information out of the network. | Covert DNS tunneling, encrypted outbound transfers, database dumps of PII/PHI. Frequently paired with double extortion. | APTs, Competitors, Organized Crime |
| **Espionage** | Secretly gathering intelligence to obtain strategic geopolitical, military, or foreign policy leverage. | Extreme stealth, long dwell times, lateral movement, targeting government communications, defense specs, and energy grids. | Nation-States, Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) |
| **Political / Philosophical** | Promoting social agendas, protesting policies, or punishing perceived corporate unethical behavior. | Website defacements, public doxxing, DDoS attacks designed to draw media attention. | Hacktivists |
| **Competitive Advantage** | Gaining an unfair business or market edge (Industrial/Corporate Espionage). | Theft of proprietary algorithms, pricing matrices, source code, patent blueprints, or merger/acquisition roadmaps. | Competitors, Corrupt Insiders, State-Backed Actors |
| **Sabotage** | Deliberately destroying, disabling, or disrupting operational systems and critical infrastructure. | Wiper malware, deleting central backups, tampering with Industrial Control Systems (ICS/SCADA), destruction of boot records. | Nation-States, Vengeful Malicious Insiders |

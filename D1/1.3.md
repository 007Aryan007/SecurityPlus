# CompTIA Security+ (SY0-701) Study Notes

## Domain 1.0: General Security Concepts (12%)

### Objective 1.3: Explain the importance of change management processes and the technical implications

## 1. Governance & Business Impact Processes

### 1.1 Purpose of Formal Change Management
* **Core Objective:** To ensure that modifications to systems, software, network architecture, and operational workflows are introduced in a controlled, coordinated, and secure manner.
* **Risk Reduction:** Prevents unintended service disruptions, configuration drift, security regressions, compliance violations, and unauthorized system modifications.
* **Standard Change Lifecycle:**
  1. **Request for Change (RFC):** Submission of proposed changes detailing rationale, scope, and technical plan.
  2. **Impact & Risk Assessment:** Evaluation of operational, security, and business dependencies.
  3. **Testing & Validation:** Verification in isolated, non-production environments.
  4. **Review & Approval:** Formal review by designated authorities/boards.
  5. **Implementation & Verification:** Controlled deployment followed by testing.
  6. **Documentation & Closeout:** Updating network maps, baselines, and configuration databases.

### 1.2 Business Impact Analysis (BIA) & Risk Assessment
* **Operational Impact:** Assessing how a maintenance activity or system patch affects revenue generation, client-facing services, internal productivity, and third-party integrations.
* **Security & Regulatory Alignment:** Verifying that the proposed change maintains compliance mandates (e.g., PCI-DSS, HIPAA, SOC 2, GDPR) and preserves established security controls (e.g., firewall ingress policies, encryption enforcement).

### 1.3 Approval Workflows & The Change Advisory Board (CAB)
* **Change Advisory Board (CAB):**
  * A multidisciplinary committee comprising stakeholders from IT operations, cybersecurity/infosec, software engineering, business units, legal/compliance, and facilities management.
  * **Core Mandate:** Reviews, evaluates, prioritizes, and authorizes non-standard changes based on technical risk, business urgency, and scheduling conflicts.
* **Emergency CAB (ECAB):**
  * A smaller, agile sub-group of the CAB convened on short notice to review and authorize critical, out-of-band updates (e.g., zero-day emergency patches or emergency disaster-recovery reconfiguration).
* **Change Types:**
  * **Standard Changes:** Low-risk, pre-approved, recurring procedures (e.g., regular workstation OS patching) following established guidelines without needing full CAB review each cycle.
  * **Normal Changes:** Moderate-to-high risk, non-routine modifications requiring formal review, documentation, testing proof, and CAB authorization.
  * **Emergency Changes:** Time-critical fixes deployed to resolve or prevent severe security incidents or operational outages.

## 2. Technical Implications & Operational Controls

### 2.1 Rollback Planning (Backout Plans)
* **Definition:** A pre-engineered, tested procedure to restore a system to its exact pre-change operational baseline if an implementation fails or introduces critical defects.
* **Requirements:**
  * Every RFC must include an explicit, step-by-step rollback strategy before receiving CAB authorization.
  * Verified recent backups (system images, database snapshots, configuration files) must be confirmed prior to beginning implementation.
  * Explicit "go / no-go" milestones and cutoff times during maintenance windows to initiate backout before production hours resume.

### 2.2 Sandboxing & Test Environments
* **Definition:** Running proposed software releases, configuration scripts, or firmware patches in dedicated, isolated environments separated from live business infrastructure.
* **Environment Progression:**
  * **Development (Dev):** Where developers write, modify, and build source code.
  * **Testing / Quality Assurance (QA):** Where functional, security, and vulnerability scanning tools validate stability and compliance.
  * **Staging / Sandbox:** An exact mirror of production architecture (identical dependencies, configurations, and mock datasets) used for final pre-deployment validation.
  * **Production (Prod):** Live enterprise environment supporting end-users and real customer data.

### 2.3 Version Control & Configuration Management
* **Version Control Systems (VCS / Git):**
  * Tracks every incremental change to source code, scripts, configuration files, and Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) templates.
  * Provides immutable commit logs: records *who* changed *what*, *when*, and *why*.
  * Enables fast reversions (checking out an earlier known-good commit) if a deployment introduces breaking changes.
* **Configuration Management Database (CMDB):**
  * Central repository storing inventory and dependency relationships of all hardware, software, licenses, and configuration baselines across the enterprise.

### 2.4 Downtime Planning & Maintenance Windows
* **Maintenance Windows:** Pre-designated, off-peak time frames (e.g., weekends or late overnight hours) scheduled for system changes to minimize user disruption.
* **High-Availability Strategies:**
  * **Blue/Green Deployment:** Two identical production environments. The "Blue" runs live traffic while changes are applied and tested on "Green." Once verified, network/router traffic switches instantly to "Green" with near-zero downtime.
  * **Canary Releases:** Rolling out the change to a tiny subset of users (e.g., 5%) before full deployment to monitor performance and catch regressions early.
* **Notification Protocols:** Timely communication to all affected internal and external stakeholders regarding anticipated service downtime and completion confirmations.

### 2.5 Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
* **Definition:** Formally documented, step-by-step instructional guides that staff must follow to execute repetitive operational tasks reliably and securely.
* **Security Benefits:**
  * Eliminates ad-hoc, error-prone configurations.
  * Ensures uniform enforcement of hardening standards and least-privilege principles.
  * Serves as standard onboarding and training documentation for IT/security personnel.
  * Ensures operational continuity during staff turnover.

## 3. Comparison Matrix: Change Management Controls

| Technical Control | Primary Purpose | Failure Consequence If Omitted |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rollback Plan** | Revert failed updates to a known-good baseline | Protracted, unrecoverable operational downtime |
| **Sandboxing** | Validate changes safely in isolated environments | Production outages caused by untested defects |
| **Version Control** | Track revisions and enable granular code/config rollbacks | Configuration drift, lost accountability, overwrite conflicts |
| **Downtime Planning** | Minimize operational disruption through scheduled windows | Unexpected service outages during critical peak hours |
| **SOPs** | Enforce consistent, repeatable execution of technical procedures | Human configuration errors, inconsistent security postures |

# CompTIA Security+ Notes: 5.5 Audits, Assessments & Training

This section focuses on how organizations verify that their security controls are actually working as intended, and how they train their human workforce—often considered the weakest link in cybersecurity.

## 1. Audits and Reporting

Audits are formal evaluations of an organization's security posture and compliance with policies or regulations.

### Internal vs. External Audits

* **Internal Audits:** Conducted by the organization's own employees (usually a dedicated internal audit team). 
  * **Purpose:** To self-assess, prepare for external audits, and continuously improve processes. They are generally less expensive but lack objective independence.
* **External Audits:** Conducted by an independent, third-party auditing firm. 
  * **Purpose:** To provide an unbiased, objective evaluation. Often legally required for regulatory compliance (like PCI-DSS or HIPAA) or demanded by business partners. 

### Attestations and SOC Reporting

* **Attestation:** A formal, written declaration by an independent auditor stating that an organization's security controls meet a specific standard. It is "proof" that you are doing what you claim to be doing.

* **SOC (Service Organization Control) Reports:** As mentioned in Section 5.4, these are critical for assessing third-party vendors. You must know the difference between the two main types of reports:
  * **Type I Report:** Evaluates the *design* of security controls at a **specific point in time**. (e.g., "On January 1st, did the company have a good firewall policy written down?"). It is faster and cheaper but less rigorous.
  * **Type II Report:** Evaluates the *operating effectiveness* of controls **over a period of time** (usually 6 to 12 months). (e.g., "From January to December, did the company actually follow its firewall policy every day?"). This is the gold standard that most clients will demand.

## 2. Security Testing

Testing is how you find the holes in your defenses before the attackers do. 

### Vulnerability Assessments vs. Penetration Testing

* **Vulnerability Assessment:**
  * **Nature:** Passive and automated.
  * **Goal:** Uses scanners (like Nessus or Qualys) to identify and log known weaknesses, missing patches, and misconfigurations.
  * **Result:** A prioritized list of vulnerabilities to fix. It **does not** attempt to exploit or weaponize the vulnerabilities.

* **Penetration Testing (Pentesting):**
  * **Nature:** Active and manual (often supplemented by tools).
  * **Goal:** A simulated cyberattack authorized by management. Pentesters actively try to **exploit** the vulnerabilities to see exactly how deep into the network they can get and what data they can steal.

### Penetration Testing Environments

Pentesters are given different levels of information before an engagement begins:

* **Black Box (Unknown Environment):** The pentester is given **zero** prior knowledge of the internal network (only the company name or target IP). 
  * *Simulates:* An external, blind attacker. Takes the longest time as the tester must do all their own reconnaissance.
* **White Box (Known Environment):** The pentester is given **full** knowledge of the network, including architecture diagrams, source code, and administrator credentials.
  * *Simulates:* A malicious insider or IT admin. Highly efficient for finding deep, complex flaws quickly.
* **Grey Box (Partially Known Environment):** A mix of both. The pentester is given the knowledge of a standard user (e.g., standard login credentials). 
  * *Simulates:* An attacker who has compromised a low-level employee's account, or a rogue standard user.

## 3. Awareness Initiatives

The best firewalls in the world cannot stop an employee from willingly handing over their password. Security awareness training is essential.

### Phishing Simulations

* **Concept:** The security team sends fake, harmless phishing emails to the company's own employees.
* **Purpose:** To see who falls for the trick and clicks the link or enters credentials. Employees who fail are usually immediately redirected to a short, mandatory training video explaining what they missed (e.g., checking the sender address or hovering over links).

### Anomalous Behavior Identification

* **Concept:** Training employees to act as human sensors for the security team. 
* **Focus:** Teaching users how to spot things that are out of the ordinary. This includes digital anomalies (a computer running extremely slowly, mouse moving on its own, unexpected MFA prompts) and physical anomalies (strangers tailgating through a secure door, someone plugging in a random USB drive).

### Secure Reporting Culture

* **Concept:** Creating a workplace environment where employees feel safe and empowered to report security incidents.
* **The Goal:** If an employee clicks a malicious link, they should feel comfortable calling the IT Helpdesk immediately. If the culture is punitive (e.g., "you will be fired if you click a bad link"), employees will hide their mistakes, giving the malware hours or days to spread across the network silently. A "blameless" reporting culture significantly speeds up Incident Response times.

# CompTIA Security+ Notes: 3.4 Resilience and Recovery

This section focuses on ensuring systems remain operational (availability) during disruptions and how an organization recovers when a disaster occurs.

## 1. High Availability (HA)

High Availability aims to eliminate Single Points of Failure (SPOF) to ensure continuous operation.

### System Redundancy

* **Load Balancing:** Distributes incoming network traffic across multiple servers. If one server fails, the load balancer stops sending traffic to it, preventing downtime and improving performance.
* **Clustering:** Grouping multiple servers to act as a single logical server.
  * **Active/Active:** All nodes are processing traffic simultaneously.
  * **Active/Passive:** One node processes traffic; the other sits idle in standby mode and takes over only if the primary fails.

### RAID (Redundant Array of Independent Disks)

RAID combines multiple hard drives to increase performance and/or provide fault tolerance.

* **RAID 0 (Striping):** Data is split across 2+ drives. **High performance, NO fault tolerance.** If one drive fails, all data is lost.
* **RAID 1 (Mirroring):** Data is identically written to 2 drives. **High fault tolerance.** If one drive fails, the other has a perfect copy. You lose 50% of your total storage capacity.
* **RAID 5 (Striping with Parity):** Requires at least 3 drives. Data and "parity" (recovery data) are striped across all drives. Can survive the loss of **one** drive.
* **RAID 6 (Striping with Double Parity):** Requires at least 4 drives. Similar to RAID 5 but with double parity. Can survive the loss of **two** drives simultaneously.
* **RAID 10 (1+0) (Stripe of Mirrors):** Requires at least 4 drives. Combines the mirroring of RAID 1 with the striping of RAID 0. Excellent performance and fault tolerance, but expensive.

### Facility Redundancy

* **Redundant Power:** 
  * **UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply):** Battery backup that provides immediate, short-term power during an outage (gives servers time to shut down gracefully or wait for generators to start).
  * **Generators:** Provide long-term power using fuel (diesel, natural gas).

## 2. Site Redundancy

If a primary data center is destroyed, organizations use alternate processing sites.

* **Hot Site:** A fully operational site with hardware, software, and real-time data replication. Recovery time is in minutes or hours. **Most expensive.**
* **Warm Site:** Has power, HVAC, and basic hardware, but data backups must be restored, and final configurations made before operations can begin. Recovery takes days. **Medium cost.**
* **Cold Site:** An empty facility with power and HVAC, but no hardware or data. You must bring in everything. Recovery takes weeks. **Cheapest option.**

## 3. Backup Strategies

Backups must be tested regularly to ensure data can actually be recovered.

### Backup Types

* **Full Backup:** Backs up *all* selected data. Takes the longest time to back up, but the fastest time to restore (only requires 1 tape/file).
* **Differential Backup:** Backs up everything that has changed **since the last Full Backup**. To restore, you need the last Full Backup + the latest Differential Backup. (Moderate backup time, moderate restore time).
* **Incremental Backup:** Backs up everything that has changed **since the last backup of ANY type**. To restore, you need the last Full Backup + *every* Incremental Backup made since then. (Fastest backup time, longest restore time).
* **Snapshots:** Captures the state of a system (usually a Virtual Machine) at a specific point in time. Allows for immediate rollback if an update or patch breaks the system.

### 3-2-1 Backup Topology

A foundational security rule for preventing complete data loss (especially against ransomware):
* Maintain **3** copies of your data (1 primary, 2 backups).
* Store them on **2** different types of media (e.g., hard drive and cloud, or disk and tape).
* Keep **1** copy completely offsite.

## 4. Continuity Planning

This involves the administrative side of resilience—the documents and plans that dictate *how* the organization responds to a crisis.

* **BIA (Business Impact Analysis):** The foundation of continuity planning. It identifies critical business processes, calculates the financial impact of downtime, and establishes crucial metrics:
  * **RTO (Recovery Time Objective):** The maximum tolerable amount of time a system can be down.
  * **RPO (Recovery Point Objective):** The maximum tolerable amount of data loss (measured in time, e.g., "we can lose up to 4 hours of data").
* **DRP (Disaster Recovery Plan):** The technical, step-by-step IT manual for recovering systems, networks, and data after a disaster has occurred.
* **COOP (Continuity of Operations Plan):** A plan heavily used in government and military environments focused on ensuring essential functions continue, often emphasizing personnel movement to alternate sites.
